Shawangunk Ridge, New York · Spring 2025

Where to study plant growth

Where could we compare growing conditions for plants on the same ridge? Find forested slopes at similar elevations, then use plant sightings to see what people have recorded nearby.

Choose sites for a field comparison

Choose two forested slopes of 10° or steeper: one north-facing (teal) and one south-facing (orange). Look for a small elevation difference and check how close each site is to a mapped trail.

For the field visit, check access and canopy at both locations. Measure light, temperature and soil moisture at the same times, then record plant growth stage.

Data sources & limits

Thin gray lines mark local town boundaries (2026 Census BAS). The slate outline marks the study area. The study boundary follows iNaturalist place 192775. The 553 public plant reports include all quality grades from April–June 2025. Observation density depends on visits and reporting effort. The data do not measure plant abundance.

Forested slopes use USGS 3DEP terrain and 2016 NLCD forest cover from Cornell’s CUGIR repository, aligned on a 30 m grid. North spans 315°–45° and south spans 135°–225°. Dashed paths show the historical state parks trail network. Exposure and trail proximity can guide a field visit; access and microclimate need field verification.

The sunlight index compares direct-beam geometry on each slope with flat ground at its latitude. It assumes open sky and uniform beam intensity. Canopy, surrounding ridges, cloud and weather can change actual plant-level light.
