Interactive sampling revision
The landscape opens a slope comparison at the selected location. The example
button uses the original detail extent. Selecting a colored slope places a pin
on the nearest original 30 m candidate cell centre of the clicked aspect and
trail-proximity class. Elevation, slope and aspect come from the original grids.
Separation uses the great-circle distance between cell centres. Slope and
bearing are rounded to whole degrees. Returning restores the overview camera.
Codex implemented this revision in response to Nicole's interaction feedback.

Guided observation comparison
The trail-side example retains the Assignment 3 camera extent. A separate
observation sequence first locates M23 and M30, then overlays their sampled
slope classes before opening exploration of all 34 observations. Dates,
attribution and reported accuracy come from the original records; elevation,
slope and aspect are rounded samples of the Assignment 3 terrain grid.
The recorded positions are 224 m apart in UTM zone 18N. Their dates are
eight days apart; flowering-stage and microclimate effects are not inferred.
Nicole selected this sequence after reviewing the overlap analysis. Codex
implemented it and prepared the two-record export from the sampled GeoPackage.

Unified map revision
The initial map combines all 553 plant observations, forest slope candidates,
relief and historical trails. Month and aspect controls act in the same view.
The terrain example changes only the camera. The two-observation comparison
is optional and returns to the previous map extent and filters.

Desktop interface revision
The month control filters reports while preserving the map camera and shows
the most frequently reported taxon name(s) in the selected period. Each
count refers to an observation ID. The bottom map key separates observation
symbols from terrain colors. Custom terrain sampling starts when the visitor
opens "Choose my own two slopes." The public trail layer remains visible.

Field comparison workflow
Forest cover (Cornell CUGIR / NLCD 2016) and the USGS 3DEP terrain grid
define candidate north- and south-facing forest cells. Field selections
sample the original 30 m cells. The elevation gap and straight-line distance
help compare candidate pairs; there is no fixed ecological suitability score.
For each cell centre the export finds the nearest original historical trail
line in UTM 18N. Line sections up to 150 m long provide a local highlight.
Distances are rounded metres to the nearest polyline, not walking distances.
The zoom example highlights mapped trail sections intersecting its extent.
No routing or verification of current access is performed.

Potential sunlight is a dimensionless terrain-only comparison for the same
selected period in 2025 (April, May, June or April–June). The index sums
positive direct-beam incidence on the inclined surface and divides by the
same site's flat-ground sum. It uses 10-minute solar-time midpoints,
daily fractional-year solar declination and each site's rounded slope and
bearing and latitude. It assumes constant beam intensity above a flat horizon.
100% denotes flat-ground exposure; scores can exceed 100%. The model omits
canopy, surrounding terrain shadows, atmosphere, clouds and diffuse light.
It is not measured insolation, temperature, moisture or plant response.
Solar geometry: NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, General Solar Position
Calculations, https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/solareqns.PDF
